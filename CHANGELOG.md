# Changelog

All notable DreamWeave changes are recorded here. DreamWeave uses a versioned, source-aware interpretive method; it is not clinical or predictive software.

## 1.0.0 — 2026-10-26

### Project launch and rebrand

- Published the skill as **DreamWeave**, a DrGekoz project authored by Joseph Williams.
- Replaced the generic analyst identity with a distinctive project name, description, metadata, installation path, README, and source index.
- Rewrote the README as a compact but detailed user-facing guide: framework inventory, reasoning method, evidence labels, example, limits, and installation.
- Removed legacy branding and upstream identity from the public project files. DreamWeave is not affiliated with any religion, cultural community, author, university, or therapy organization.

### Research and interpretive framework

- Established a seven-stage reasoning method: preserve the report; begin with personal associations; establish an evidence floor; interpret each requested framework separately; label the claim; compare overlaps and disagreements; synthesize without inflating certainty.
- Added explicit evidence classes for empirical findings, supported associations, emerging hypotheses, psychoanalytic theory, religious/philosophical belief, myth/folklore, literary analogy, modern esotericism, pseudohistorical/pseudoscientific claims, and personal speculation.
- Added the governing principle **“Convergence raises salience, not proof.”** Apparent cross-cultural agreement must be checked for source transmission, shared experience, selection, translation, broad wording, and hindsight. Similarity alone does not establish objective, supernatural, or predictive truth.
- Added separate framework readings, a cross-framework common-threads matrix, a Differences Summary, and an optional “Joseph’s Interpretation.” The synthesis distinguishes evidence, psychological possibility, cultural resonance, and unknowns.
- Added guards against fixed universal symbol dictionaries, fabricated cultural meanings, unsupported prophecy, diagnosis, repressed-memory claims, and inferring another person's intent from a dream.

### Framework coverage

The README counts 15 broad categories, with named subtraditions and source-specific lenses:

1. Empirical dream science and clinical sleep research.
2. Freud and psychoanalysis.
3. Jung, analytical psychology, and *The Red Book*.
4. Daoist/Taoist philosophy and the *Zhuangzi* butterfly dream.
5. Buddhist traditions and Tibetan dream yoga.
6. Chinese folklore, dream manuals, and divination.
7. Hindu philosophical dream perspectives.
8. Jewish Biblical and rabbinic traditions.
9. Christian Biblical and later interpretive traditions.
10. Islamic dream traditions.
11. Norse/Viking-era saga literature.
12. Ancient Egyptian and ancient Near Eastern texts.
13. Greek and Roman traditions, including Artemidorus.
14. Bounded, source-attributed African and Indigenous examples, including Amerindian, Aboriginal Australian, and Māori scholarship.
15. Tolkien as literature, plus clearly dated modern esoteric literature.

Additional provenance-critical treatments:

- Egyptian papyri are distinguished from later Hermetic sources.
- The medieval Arabic/Latin *Emerald Tablet* is distinguished from M. Doreal's modern *Emerald Tablets of Thoth the Atlantean* (1939 bibliographic record); unsupported Atlantean provenance is not presented as ancient Egyptian fact.
- 1 Enoch's ancient Jewish apocalyptic Dream Visions are distinguished from modern prediction claims and from 2/3 Enoch; canon status is qualified by community.
- Anunna/Anunnaki are described as deities in ancient Sumerian/Akkadian sources; modern alien-visitor claims are not conflated with those texts or treated as established archaeology.
- Tolkien's fiction is clearly a literary lens, not a clinical theory or historical revelation.

### Research scope and limitations

- Added linked research modules for the science framework, comparative traditions, output protocol, research checklist, and known gaps, alongside Freud, Jung/*The Red Book*, Biblical, and Tolkien material.
- Recorded sources in `SKILL.md` and the modules. Source type and claims are differentiated; primary religious texts document their own traditions, not scientific validation of metaphysical claims.
- Documented that this is a selective, source-led synthesis, **not an expert-reviewed systematic review**. No external experts, community representatives, or clinicians reviewed this release. It does not claim to represent all global traditions.
- Kept the project “All rights reserved.” Public visibility does not itself grant reuse rights. No external skill repository or organization endorses DreamWeave.

### Verification

- Reviewed the skill structure, module index, citations, and local Markdown links before publishing.
- Hermes skill loader reports the local skill available; this check verifies loading, not clinical validity or the correctness of every historical interpretation.

### Known limits

- Historical and living traditions require further specialist and community review; summaries are necessarily selective.
- Research on dream functions and report interpretation remains methodologically constrained; dream science does not establish fixed symbolic meanings.
- The synthesis protocol is an original reflective procedure, not a validated psychometric instrument or diagnostic method.
- Recheck links, source editions/translations, and current clinical recommendations before future releases.
