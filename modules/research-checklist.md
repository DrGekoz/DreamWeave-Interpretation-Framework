# Research Checklist — Expanded Comparative Review

**Completed:** 2026-10-26. **Status:** substantial selective review; NOT a systematic review, exhaustive global survey, or expert-reviewed document. The distinction is intentional: do not describe this work as an expert-reviewed systematic review.

## Scope and process

- Searched current sleep/dream science reviews, experimental studies, cross-cultural comparisons, nightmare-care guidance, specialist scholarship on psychoanalysis, and primary/institutional sources for selected religious, folkloric, and literary traditions.
- Reviewed sources on empirical research; Freud; Jung and *The Red Book*; biblical and rabbinic Judaism/Christianity; Daoist philosophy; Buddhist narrative/contemplative traditions; early Chinese divination/popular dream interpretation; Hindu philosophy; Islam; Norse saga literature; ancient Near Eastern/Egyptian/Greek/Roman traditions; African, Amerindian, Australian Aboriginal, and Māori examples; Tolkien as literature; 1 Enoch's Dream Visions; the historical Emerald Tablet versus the modern Doreal *Emerald Tablets of Thoth the Atlantean*; and Mesopotamian Anunna/Anunnaki traditions versus modern ancient-astronaut claims.
- Research performed via targeted web searches and source pages; no preregistered protocol, database-by-database search, duplicate screening, formal risk-of-bias assessment across the entire corpus, or external expert review. The 2025 neuropsychoanalysis meta-review is itself systematic for its narrow neuroanatomical scope; it does not make this skill review systematic.
- Sources include peer-reviewed articles/reviews, academic books/chapters, institutional pages, primary religious texts, and some historical editions. Each claim must be weighted by source type; an institutional page is not automatically peer-reviewed, and a religious text documents a tradition rather than scientifically validating its metaphysics.

## Checklist

1. **Freshness/risk:** completed. Dream interpretation has significant epistemic and mental-health risk; treatment claims require clinical sources. Reviewed material available through 2025; recheck before future clinical guidance.
2. **Science search:** completed selectively. Added 2025 dream-science review and neuropsychoanalytic meta-review, 2024 methodological reviews/experiment, cross-cultural study, 2023 systematic nightmare-treatment review, AASM position paper, and 2024 clinical consensus.
3. **Framework coverage:** expanded substantially across the named traditions above. Added specific provenance distinctions for Egyptian/Hermetic texts, Enochic apocalypse, and Mesopotamian deities. Broad headings are explicitly qualified; no claim to represent all schools, cultures, or living communities.
4. **Evidence labels:** added formal taxonomy separating empirical evidence, psychoanalysis, religious/philosophical belief, myth/folklore/literature, and speculation. Explicitly rejects “cross-cultural repetition proves truth.”
5. **Legacy guidance:** replaced universal-symbol and mystical-first assumptions. Retained traditions only as attributed comparative lenses; prioritized the dreamer's own associations and context.
6. **Sources/metadata:** citations and access date recorded in topic modules and skill metadata; confidence medium.
7. **Uncertainties:** documented in known-gaps.md, including incomplete 2025–26 search and lack of expert review.
8. **Output contract:** added full interpretation schema with science lens, framework-by-framework analysis, cross-framework convergence, Differences Summary, and Joseph's Interpretation.

## Validation rule

Do not claim expert review or systematic-review completeness. To upgrade this status, define a narrow review question, search named databases (e.g., PubMed, PsycINFO, Scopus/Web of Science, ATLA/Religious Studies, specialist area databases), log exact queries/date/eligibility, screen duplicates and full texts, evaluate source quality, include language/coverage limitations, and obtain review from relevant sleep scientist, clinical nightmare specialist, historians of religions, and tradition-specific scholars/community authorities. A single “all frameworks worldwide” systematic review is not a tractable or methodologically coherent question; break it into scoped questions.
