# Biblical Dream Traditions

## A diverse religious text, not a single dream dictionary

Biblical books portray dreams in varied ways. Some narratives present dreams as divine communication; other passages caution against false claims, treat dreams as ordinary mental activity, or distinguish dreams from trustworthy revelation. These are theological-literary portrayals, not evidence that a modern dream is divine or predictive. Respect the user's faith and denomination; do not claim to speak for God.

## Useful textual examples

- **Genesis 28:10–22:** Jacob's dream at Bethel and his response.
- **Genesis 37:5–11:** Joseph's dreams and the family conflict around their interpretation.
- **Genesis 40–41:** Joseph interprets officials' and Pharaoh's dreams; the narrative places interpretation in a theological frame.
- **Numbers 12:6:** a biblical statement about prophetic communication through visions and dreams.
- **Job 33:14–18:** dreams described as one possible mode of warning/instruction in Elihu's speech; attend to speaker and context.
- **1 Kings 3:5–15:** Solomon's dream at Gibeon, framed as a divine encounter and request for wisdom.
- **Daniel 2 and 4; Daniel 7:** courtly dream interpretation and symbolic visions in historical/apocalyptic contexts.
- **Ecclesiastes 5:3, 7 (numbering varies):** dreams associated with much activity and caution about empty words.
- **Deuteronomy 13:1–5 and Jeremiah 23:25–32:** warnings against false prophetic authority and misleading dream claims.
- **Matthew 1:20–24; 2:12–22:** dreams guide Joseph and the Magi within Matthew's narrative.

## Responsible faith-sensitive response

Ask whether the dreamer wants a Biblical reading and which tradition/translation they prefer. Discuss a specific passage's narrative function and imagery, then ask what it means within the dreamer's faith. A parallel to Joseph, Daniel, Jacob, or Matthew is a literary/theological comparison, not proof of divine instruction. Encourage discernment, community guidance, and scriptural context rather than confident AI claims. Do not pressure religious belief or dismiss it.

## Method note

This is a compact cross-reference, not an exhaustive account of Jewish and Christian interpretive traditions, historical reception, languages, or denominations. For detailed exegesis, check the primary text in the requested translation and reputable academic commentaries representing relevant traditions. Do not treat one secondary summary as consensus.
